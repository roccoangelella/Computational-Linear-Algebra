GMRES minimizes norm(beta e1-Hbar_m y). Apply an orthogonal transform Q_m, chosen so Q_m Hbar_m=[R_m;0], with upper-triangular R_m. Orthogonality preserves norm and Q_m(beta e1)=[g_m;gamma_(m+1)]. Thus squared residual is norm(g_m-R_m y)^2+abs(gamma_(m+1))^2. When R_m is nonsingular, solve the small triangular system R_m y=g_m and minimum residual norm is abs(gamma_(m+1)). This is preferred over normal equations for numerical stability. Q_m is the QR-side row transformation, distinct from Arnoldi basis V_m. Continuing numeric example Hbar2=[[3,1],[1,3],[0,sqrt2]], beta e1=[sqrt2,0,0]. Next part builds Q_m with Givens rotations.
