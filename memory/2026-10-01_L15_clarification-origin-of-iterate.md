topics clarified:
- An iterative method does not already know an approximation x^(k) before starting. It begins from an explicitly chosen initial guess x^(0).
- The initial guess can often be arbitrary for the theoretical convergence discussion; common simple choices include the zero vector. Better application-specific guesses can accelerate convergence, but are not required to define the method.
- From the splitting A=M-N, the exact solution satisfies the fixed-point equation x=M^{-1}N x+M^{-1}b.
- The iteration is defined by repeatedly applying this fixed-point map: x^(k+1)=M^{-1}N x^(k)+M^{-1}b.
- Thus x^(1) is generated from x^(0), x^(2) from x^(1), and in general x^(k) is the approximation generated by the previous k applications of the rule.
- Replacing the x on the right-hand side by x^(k) is not an algebraic identity; it is the algorithmic approximation step that defines the fixed-point iteration.
- Whether this sequence actually approaches the exact solution is a separate question answered by the convergence condition, later expressed through the iteration matrix B=M^{-1}N and spectral radius rho(B)<1.
