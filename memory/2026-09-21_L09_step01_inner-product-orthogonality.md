topics studied:
- For real vectors, the standard inner product is <x,y>=x^T y, i.e. the sum of componentwise products.
- The Euclidean norm is induced by the inner product: ||x||_2=sqrt(<x,x>).
- Two vectors are orthogonal exactly when their inner product is zero.
- Orthogonality generalizes the geometric idea of perpendicular directions.
- A unit vector has Euclidean norm 1.
- A set of vectors is orthonormal when the vectors are mutually orthogonal and each has norm 1.
- If Q has orthonormal columns, then Q^T Q=I; diagonal entries encode unit norms and off-diagonal entries encode pairwise orthogonality.
- This structure is the basis for Gram-Schmidt, QR factorization, orthogonal projection and least squares.
